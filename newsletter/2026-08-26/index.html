OpenAI built a chip. The catch is the timing.

OpenAI showed its own chip and said it beats Nvidia's current inference boxes. Nvidia reports earnings tonight. The catch is the timing.

01

OpenAI's Jalapeño chip posted its first benches. It claims to beat Blackwell on inference

At Hot Chips on Tuesday, OpenAI shared InferenceX results for Jalapeño, the Broadcom-built inference ASIC. Head of hardware Richard Ho said it serves more work per watt and answers faster than current state of the art. On GPT-OSS 120B it showed about 1.9x higher peak mixed tokens per second per kilowatt and about 1.7x lower end-to-end latency versus the Nvidia GB200 comparison, at 700 watts against GB200's 1,200. DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K2.5 showed similar 1.5x to 1.7x efficiency gains, with up to 3.6x lower latency. These are OpenAI's numbers, on a public SemiAnalysis benchmark, against Blackwell-generation boxes, not Vera Rubin. Ho said very small volumes at the end of 2026, more in 2027. Codex was already running on the chip earlier this year.

SourcesTechCrunch, Aug 25ServeTheHome, Aug 25

02

Nvidia reports earnings tonight. Custom silicon is the question hanging over the call

Nvidia reports after the close, around 4:20 p.m. ET. Jalapeño dropped the day before. The comparison is GB200 and GB300, not Rubin, and the chip is inference-only and not in volume yet. SemiAnalysis's Dylan Patel still called first-gen custom silicon beating Blackwell "huge news." Watch whether Jensen addresses customer chips unprompted.
